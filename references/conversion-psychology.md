# Screenshot conversion psychology

Why this matters: on the App Store search results page, the first two portrait
screenshots render side by side at roughly 25–30% of full size. Most users never
tap through to the listing page. The decision to install or scroll past is made
against those two thumbnails and the icon. Everything below follows from that.

## The slot framework

Treat the set as an argument in sequence, not a feature gallery.

| Slot | Job | Failure mode |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The hook. One promise, stated as an outcome the reader wants. | Showing a login screen, a dashboard, or the app's name. |
| 2 | The core loop. What the user actually does, daily. | A second abstract promise with no mechanism behind it. |
| 3 | The differentiator. Why this app and not the free alternative. | A feature any competitor also has. |
| 4–5 | Depth. The features that retain, not the ones that acquire. | Front-loading these and burying the hook. |
| 6+ | Proof and close. Real testimonials, real numbers, privacy posture. | Fabricated proof. |

Slots 1 and 2 are worth more than 3–10 combined. Spend effort accordingly.

## Principles that change behavior

**Outcome over mechanism.** People buy the after-state. "Know what to say when
it matters" sells; "AI-generated conversation prompts" describes. Name the
mechanism only when it *is* the differentiator.

**One idea per image.** At thumbnail size the eye resolves one message. Two
captions, or a caption plus an annotated callout, collapse into noise.

**Caption above device.** Text placed above the phone frame is read first and
survives cropping. Text inside the device screen is unreadable at 30%.

**Contrast carries further than detail.** A caption in high contrast against a
flat background reads at any size. Gradients behind text, low-contrast pastels,
and thin weights disappear in thumbnails.

**Specificity beats superlatives.** "A new question every morning" outperforms
"The best relationship app". Numbers are credible; adjectives are not.

**Show the artifact, not the empty state.** A screen full of plausible real
content signals a working product. Empty states signal an unfinished one — even
when the empty state is beautifully designed.

**Faces and emotion work for consumer apps.** Human presence raises engagement
for social, health, and relationship categories. It does the opposite for
developer tools and finance, where it reads as stock imagery.

**Sequence for momentum.** Each caption should make the next one feel inevitable.
Read the captions aloud as a list with the screens hidden — if it doesn't hold
together as a paragraph, the order is wrong.

## Reading competitors' screenshot sets

Look at the top-ranking sets for patterns, not to copy:

- **Count the panels.** Leaders often use all 10 (Apple) or 8 (Play), but most users never pass slot 3.
- **Slot 1 type.** Outcome headline over the hero screen? A lifestyle photo? A panoramic set spanning panels? If every leader does the same thing, a clearly different slot 1 stands out in the grid, as long as it's still legible.
- **Proof placement.** Where do ratings, awards and press appear? Only use proof you really have.
- **Colour.** If the category is a sea of one colour, a contrasting background is free visibility.

## Copy patterns that convert

- **Second person, present tense.** "You'll never blank again."
- **Curiosity gap.** State the outcome, withhold the mechanism, so the screen
  underneath answers the question the caption raised.
- **Loss framing, used once.** "Stop forgetting what matters to them." Powerful,
  but it makes the whole set feel anxious if repeated.
- **Concrete nouns.** "Anniversary, allergy, her sister's name" outperforms
  "important details".

Avoid: "Welcome to X", "Powered by AI", "Simple. Beautiful. Fast.", any caption
that names the app instead of what it does for the reader.

## Localization

Caption length varies wildly across languages — German and Finnish commonly run
30–40% longer than English. Design the layout to survive the longest target
language, or the caption wraps into the device frame and the panel breaks. Never
ship an English screenshot with a translated caption; the mismatch reads as
machine translation and depresses conversion in that storefront.

## Honesty constraints

These are not stylistic preferences.

- Social proof must be real. Invented ratings, review quotes, download counts,
  and press logos are grounds for App Review rejection and are a lie to the user.
- Screenshots must depict the submitted build. Mocked-up features that do not
  exist yet are a rejection reason under App Store Review Guideline 2.3.
- No real person's likeness or private data, and no competitor trademarks.
